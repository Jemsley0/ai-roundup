# Special edition: the AI economy and its public, October 2026

This special edition covers the economics of AI and its public reception from mid-August to 2026-10-17, weighted to the last 30 days. It is one argued page, not a news list, and it sits beside the [[2026-10-17]] daily note rather than replacing it. Every figure carries its source. Where a number is vendor-reported, second-hand or unconfirmed on a first-party page, the sentence says so.

## The short version

This is the edition's own read, tested against the evidence below.

- Open-weight models carry most tokens on the public gateways but a small share of the spend, so the volume is spread out and the dollars are concentrated.
- The two leading labs still take most business spend, and Anthropic's growth has slowed from above 100% a quarter to about 45%, and no comparable quarterly figure is public for OpenAI.
- Consumer reach is huge and payment is thin: at the last confirmed count, about 5.5% of ChatGPT's weekly users paid.
- The stress sits in the financing: the official sector now worries less about valuations than about debt and circular deals, and the stock market has not yet agreed.
- The buildout is meeting political limits: local opposition to data centers runs at 60% to 75%, public concern keeps rising, and export controls in both directions now reach models, not only chips.

One part of the thesis holds less well than the rest. Tokens are cheap per task, but rental prices for the chips that make them rose through 2026.

## The money: lab revenue, losses and valuations

**The two leading labs are still growing faster than any public company of their size, but Anthropic's growth is slowing, neither lab's numbers are audited, and the headline gap between them is mostly accounting.**

The confirmed floor is thin. Anthropic's own Series H post (2026-05-28) gives \$65B raised at \$965B post-money and a run rate that "crossed \$47 billion" in May ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)). The only Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) text on its spending is a counterparty's. Nscale's registration statement (S-1), filed 2026-09-18, says Anthropic's agreements with it provide for payments "up to approximately \$44.6 billion" ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002110365/000119312526395475/ck0002110365-20260918.htm)).

Everything above that floor is second-hand. Anthropic told investors on 2026-10-14 that Q3 revenue was \$16.8B, up about 45% from \$11.6B in Q2, after more than 100% growth the quarter before. That figure comes from investor-day attendees, relayed by Reuters through Calcalist, and no first-party or SEC page carries it ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/tgb6eqw4z)). Anthropic's leaked draft S-1, reported by Reuters and read here through a secondary site, shows 2025 revenue of about \$4.6B and a \$42B net loss, of which about \$34B is non-cash. It also shows at least \$518B of multi-year compute commitments, about 80% of them non-cancellable, and cloud marketplaces carrying about 47% of 2025 usage revenue ([AI Lately](https://ailately.com/articles/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-economics)). The \$1.5T to \$2T valuation talk for the initial public offering (IPO), with a listing likely after the 2026-11-03 midterms, rests on secondary reports only. [[2026-09-21]] covered the slip to November.

OpenAI told investors its annualised revenue was "approaching \$50 billion" at the end of September, per the FT as summarised by Dealroom, not the roughly \$70B reported on 2026-09-29 ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/161093-openais-annualised-revenue-is-about-20b-below-the-70b-reported-ft-says/)). The \$20B gap is gross versus net: Anthropic counts sales through its cloud partners at full value and OpenAI does not, so investors had regrossed OpenAI's number to compare the two. OpenAI is in early talks to raise at least \$30B at about \$1.4T before the new money, with no term sheet signed, per Bloomberg and The Information ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-valuation-1-4tn-30bn-round-revenue-70bn)).

The thesis says growth is slowing from extreme rates. That holds for Anthropic. No comparable quarterly growth figure for OpenAI is public, so the thesis is unproven there.

The strongest pushback is that 45% a quarter is still extraordinary. Anthropic booked about \$33B in nine months against \$4.6B in all of 2025, and told investors it was "profitable on an adjusted basis" in Q3. The sceptics answer that "adjusted" carries the claim. Deep Quarry's Olga Usvyatsky called the profit "perhaps almost too good to be true" ([AI Lately](https://ailately.com/articles/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-economics)). An 85% adjusted gross margin appears on only one page, and Calcalist says margins were not reported ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/tgb6eqw4z)).

Read: the revenue is real and compounding, but until a public S-1 shows audited margins, the valuations price an adjusted profit that nobody outside the investor room can check.

## Is it a bubble? The official sector moves from valuations to financing

**The official sector has largely stopped asking whether AI stocks are too expensive and started asking how the buildout is financed, and who holds the debt if revenue disappoints.**

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) set the frame on 2026-10-13. Its October Global Financial Stability Report (GFSR) names an AI-driven correction as one of three salient risks, beside higher government borrowing costs and a major cyberattack. Its blog counts 14 disclosed "circular" deals since 2023, in which about \$300B of equity and about \$650B of debt flowed into AI firms in return for about \$1.4T of future revenue commitments ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2026/10/12/global-financial-markets-face-rising-risks-from-ai-bond-yields-and-cyberattacks)). [[2026-10-17]] carried the headline. Unite.AI and The Next Web report a further IMF figure: hyperscalers now supply about 10% of global investment-grade bond issuance, up from under 1% in 2024. That figure is not on any IMF page that loaded ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/imf-circular-ai-deals-financial-stability-report)). Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said "We now have an economy that is standing on one leg, the AI leg." The IMF's own AI-bust scenario, as the Kuwait Times reports it, cuts global gross domestic product (GDP) 1.1% by 2028 without a global recession ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/51532/business/imf-flags-risks-to-global-economy-from-ai-mideast-war-and-debt/)).

Other official bodies point the same way. The Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee record of 2026-07-07 says AI firms' use of credit markets "including in public markets, private credit, leveraged and structured finance" has accelerated rapidly. It names "the depreciation rate of data centre facilities and AI chips" as a key assumption ([Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/bank-of-england-financial-policy-committee-record-july-2026-202677/)). The Bank for International Settlements wrote in January that AI financing is shifting "from operating cash flows to debt" ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull120.htm)).

The stress has a first address. Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the Blue Owl unit that developed Project Jupiter, the New Mexico Stargate campus, to secure the right to defer rent if power problems delay a 2028 opening. That is Bloomberg, via The Next Web ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/oracle-force-majeure-project-jupiter-new-mexico)). About \$18B of loans on the campus traded at 89 to 91 cents, per the FT via Reuters ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/19/oracle039s-18-billion-data-center-debt-under-pressure-ft-reports)). The 2026-10-17 note covered banks trying to offload other data center debt ([[2026-10-17]]). Nvidia-backed Firmus scrapped a roughly \$5B Australian IPO on 2026-10-09 ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/09/nvidia-backed-firmus-scraps-5-billion-australian-ipo-on)).

The bull case is thin in this research. No dated first-party bull argument with reach turned up in the window. What exists is the market's answer, not an argument. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed at records on 2026-10-06, and Nvidia approached a \$6T valuation ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/sp-500-nasdaq-record-highs-tech-stocks/)). The bust scenario is also mild: 1.1% of GDP over two years is a slowdown, not 2008. Oracle wants to defer rent, not leave the lease ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/oracle-force-majeure-project-jupiter-new-mexico)).

Against that, the US 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.24% on 2026-10-01 ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/05/the-2-trillion-private-credit-market-just-got-some-good-news-but-its-biggest-test-may-still-be-coming/)). Higher long rates raise the cost of exactly the long-dated paper hyperscalers now issue.

Read: this edition reads the cycle as a leveraged buildout with a funding risk rather than a bust in progress, and interest rates, not AI demand, are the variable to watch.

## Open weights and frontier labs: tokens versus dollars

**Open-weight models now carry most tokens on the public routing gateways, but paying businesses still buy almost entirely from Anthropic and OpenAI.**

So, to answer the question directly: are open-weight models taking share? Of tokens on public gateways, yes. Of business dollars, the evidence says not much yet.

The token data is first-party and recent. OpenRouter's 2026-09-22 newsletter says open-weight token share crossed 50% durably in April, peaked at 79% in early July and "has stayed above 62% since" ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/data/newsletter/the-token-state-of-play)). Its December 2025 paper put open-weight share at about one-third in late 2025. That is the earlier baseline, but it covers a different period with a different method ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/state-of-ai)). Vercel's AI Gateway reports open-weight models at 56% of August tokens but 14% of spend, estimated at list prices, with Anthropic alone at 64% of spend ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/ai-gateway-production-index-september-2026)). OpenRouter charts spend by model type but does not publish the split as a number. Both gateways are vendor-reported, and both measure developer and startup routing, not enterprise contracts.

Purchase data points the other way. Ramp's card and bill-pay data shows 43.5% of US businesses paid Anthropic and 39.7% paid OpenAI in July ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/anthropic-vs-openai-business-adoption-2026-ramp-data)). From Ramp's figures, McMahon and Konstantin Pilz calculate that at least 77% of AI-paying Ramp businesses use one of the two ([Digg](https://digg.com/ai/fyb4qfzt)). Ramp cannot see open models bought through a hyperscaler or run on a company's own hardware, so it understates open use. No 2026 Menlo Ventures or a16z enterprise-share page was found.

Three things explain the gap between tokens and dollars. The first is price: Artificial Analysis puts one index task at about \$0.13 on MiMo-V2.6-Pro against about \$6 on Opus 5.5. The second is quality: on version 4.3 of the same index, the best open model sits 12 points behind the best closed one ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/claude-opus-5-5-takes-the-top-spot-on-artificial-analysis-at-58/)). The third is volume. OpenRouter's 2026-10-08 edition says agentic keys used about 7x the tokens of human keys, and that cached prompts were just under 90% of agentic tokens ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/data/newsletter/cache-is-crucial)). Token share therefore overstates economic weight.

Share loss in dollars would matter for lab valuations. If open models moved from the volume tier into business spend, the closed labs would have to cut flagship prices. In investor Gavin Baker's framing, the margin would then move to chips, power and hyperscalers ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/gavin-baker-explains-why-kimi-k3-is-negative-for-openai-anthropic-but-positive-for-nearly-every-company-in-the-world/)). The counterpoint is that open weights have not started a price war. DeepSeek raised prices 2.3x to 4.5x and doubled its run rate to about \$1B with no reported customer loss, per The Information via Dealroom ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/info-1jq5etc-deepseeks-annualized-revenue-hits-1-billion-a)). The closed labs defend the volume tier with cheap small models instead.

The pushback on "not much yet" is the trend. Two independent gateways show a steep and sustained rise. An Enterprise Technology Research survey of 200 buyers, funder undisclosed, puts open models at 34% of enterprise tokens, up from 23% a year earlier, though it says they "still attract less enterprise spending" ([Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/articles/open-weight-ai-models-expected-to-capture-41-of-enterprise-token-usage/)).

Read: open weights have won the volume tier, and the test is whether an open-weight vendor appears in paid business data such as Ramp's.

## Consumers: reach without payment

**Consumer AI reaches about a billion people, but few of them pay, and the business models built on free use are under strain.**

Reach is not in doubt. Sundar Pichai said on 2026-08-11 that Gemini passed 1B monthly users, and Google named no paid-subscriber count ([Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/google-puts-gemini-at-1-billion-monthly-users-without-naming-paying-subscribers/)). A claim that ChatGPT passed 1B weekly users circulated from late summer, but its date is disputed and it was not confirmed first-party. The last confirmed figure is 50M paying subscribers out of 900M weekly users on 2026-02-27, about 5.5% ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/chatgpt-reaches-900m-weekly-active-users)).

Meta's Muse agent, launched 2026-09-08 ([[2026-09-09]]), is the live test of habit. Sensor Tower estimates, relayed by Deedy Das, put Muse's day-1 retention at 73.3% against ChatGPT's 41.3%, and day-14 retention at 22.5% against 26.8% ([Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15719956544553984)). Hunterbrook Capital, which disclosed a short position in Meta, says net user growth fell 62.4% from the first surge ([Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15735512965932032)). Meta has published no usage figures. Its own 2026-10-12 post says the new Muse Wonder model will roll out "first to subscribers", so Meta now uses model quality to sell the paid tier ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/introducing-muse-wonder-models/)). Earlier notes logged the trust objections and Amazon's block ([[2026-10-01]], [[2026-10-02]]).

Character.AI shows the floor. It filed for Chapter 11 on 2026-10-12 ([[2026-10-17]]). From the CEO's first-day declaration, PPC Land calculates August revenue of about \$0.44 per active user a month against about \$0.58 of compute. The filing itself was not reached ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/character-ai-loses-8m-a-month-despite-2-5m-in-monthly-ad-sales/)). High engagement raised costs faster than revenue.

Ads are the industry's answer to weak conversion. OpenAI says ChatGPT Ads reached a \$1B annualised run rate, reported 2026-08-31, which is one month multiplied by 12 ([Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/chatgpt-ads-hits-1-billion-run-rate-but-advertisers-are-not-sold/)). The advertiser evidence is weaker. Adthena measured 0.91% click-through for one brand against 6.4% on Google search. Agency executives report about 0.6% and no conversions, which is anecdote.

The pushback is that people pay for products they value. Sensor Tower says 13% of Claude's users pay, and US mobile revenue per Claude user reached \$2.76 in May ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/press/sensor-tower-state-of-ai-2026-report-global-time-spent-on-generative-ai-apps-projected-to-more-than-double-year-over-year)). Day-14 retention of 22.5% is not a collapse, and the Muse fade comes from a short seller and data vendors with undisclosed methods.

Read: consumer AI has solved distribution and not payment, and for the free tier the bet is now ads, not subscriptions.

## Data centers: the buildout becomes local politics

**Local opposition to data centers is a majority in every national poll, and the fight has moved from county boards to governors, the Senate floor and midterm advertising.**

The lead number is a repeated question. Heatmap Pro and Embold asked voters whether they would oppose a data center in their area. In August 2025, 42% said yes; in a poll fielded 2026-08-08 to 2026-08-13, 75% did ([Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/daily/data-centers-unpopular-fracking)). Other national polls land between 60% and 75%. UMass Amherst, fielded 2026-08-21 to 2026-08-26, found 65% opposed and 11% in favour, with Republicans opposed 52% to 18% ([UMass](https://www.umass.edu/poll/about/reports/2026-09-national-public-opinion-poll-2)). Annenberg found 61%, up from 49% in the spring, per Decrypt ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/377162?amp=1)). The Gallup, Pew and Annenberg first-party pages did not load, so their figures come through the pages that did.

Governors of both parties acted. Republican Greg Abbott ordered a grid-interconnection audit and pause in Texas on 2026-08-03 ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/08/data-center-approvals-texas-halted-until-audits-completed-gov-greg-abbott-says/415194/)). Democrat Kathy Hochul signed New York's Executive Order 62 on 2026-07-14, pausing discretionary permits for up to a year ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/es/insights/2026/07/new-york-enacts-first-statewide-data-center-moratorium)).

Congress agreed on the complaint and not the fix. The House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417 to 3 on 2026-09-16 ([[2026-09-17]]). On 2026-09-30 the Senate rejected cloture 57 to 43, in roll call 254 on H.R. 9340 ([Senate](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00254.htm)). The match between that bill number and the act's name comes from Roll Call, not the Senate page, which also reports that Democrats called the bill voluntary and "toothless" ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/09/30/voluntary-ratepayer-protection-bill-blocked-in-senate-again/)). Three Democratic senators then said seven developers resist paying for grid upgrades ([[2026-10-12]]).

The issue is now paid media. AdImpact counts \$86.5M in ads mentioning data centers since July, in 38 states and almost all negative, via Bisnow ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/data-center-community-relations/data-centers-have-become-a-tv-villain-on-the-midterm-campaign-trail)).

The price evidence is split, and both sides are measured. The market monitor for PJM, the largest US grid market, attributes 9.7% of first-half 2026 wholesale cost to data center demand in the capacity market. That figure is read through a secondary page ([MyChesCo](https://www.mychesco.com/a/news/regional/pjm-power-costs-jump-50-as-data-centers-drive-capacity-surge/)). A Rutgers study of 2026-06-22 finds no clear local bill effect up to 2024 ([Rutgers](https://policylab.rutgers.edu/?p=11153)). The two measure different things: regional capacity prices driven by forecast load, and retail bills near older facilities. [[2026-10-09]] logged four more papers that disagree. Voters have settled the question anyway: 53% told Heatmap in May that data centers helped raise power prices ([Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/daily/data-center-polling)).

The pushback is that opposition has not become law. Even a voluntary cost bill failed in the Senate, and the measured effect on local bills is small.

Read: the likely end state is "pay your own way" rules on grid costs and tax breaks, not national moratoria, and local consent is now as hard a limit on the buildout as chips.

## Public sentiment

**Public concern about AI keeps rising across ages and parties, and it is running ahead of the measured harm.**

The polls agree on direction. Quinnipiac, fielded 2026-09-24 to 2026-09-27 with 1,202 adults, found 77% want powerful AI stopped (30%) or slowed (47%). It found 73% concerned about a threat to human survival and 86% in favour of independent safety standards ([Quinnipiac](https://poll.qu.edu/poll-release?releaseid=3969)). Economist/YouGov, fielded 2026-10-02 to 2026-10-05, found 71% of registered voters say AI moves "too quickly": 82% of Democrats, 74% of independents and 59% of Republicans ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55700-democrats-8-point-lead-with-registered-voters-inflation-importance-hits-new-high-and-more-october-25-2026-economist-yougov-poll)). AP-NORC found 64% ([[2026-10-08]]). CBS/YouGov's 55% stop-or-slow figure rests on Tech Policy Press, whose comparison runs from 55% to 77% and notes the wordings differ ([Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/multiple-polls-find-most-americans-think-ai-is-moving-too-fast)). The Ipsos page did not load.

Pew gives the longest series. In its June survey of 3,488 adults, 52% were more concerned than excited, up from 37% in 2021 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/08/18/young-adults-in-the-us-are-increasingly-wary-of-ai-concerned-it-will-take-jobs/)). By level, adults 65 and over are the most concerned, at 59%. The youth story is the pace of change. Concern among under-30s rose from 31% to 55%, so for the first time a majority of them are more concerned than excited.

Fear is ahead of the labour data. Stanford's Digital Economy Lab, using ADP payroll data, finds employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs about 19% below its counterfactual as of June 2026, up from 15% ([Stanford](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)). The figure is descriptive, not causal, and the authors say so: "We do not see widespread, economy-wide job displacement". Fairlie and Wu found recent-graduate unemployment inside its normal range, and Challenger's AI share of US layoffs fell to about 9% in September ([[2026-10-02]]). Anthropic's study of its own staff shows junior hiring falling, but it covers one firm ([[2026-10-17]]).

Resistance is becoming policy in small places. The QGIS project merged a ban on AI-assisted contributions on 2026-10-07 on maintainer-load grounds, and the Hacker News thread drew 392 points ([GitHub](https://github.com/qgis/QGIS-Enhancement-Proposals/pull/387)).

The pushback is that a pace question is cheap to answer, and nothing in the window shows the opinion changing behaviour.

Read: concern is now broad and cross-party, which makes AI an easy target before the midterms, but the only measured harm sits in entry-level hiring, and no study yet shows that AI caused it.

## Geopolitics: export control reaches software

**Export controls now run in both directions and reach models, not only chips.**

The anchor is American and predates the window. On 2026-06-12 a Commerce directive suspended foreign-national access to Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5, and Anthropic disabled both for everyone. Anthropic said "we disagree that the finding of a narrow potential jailbreak should be cause for recalling a commercial model" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access)). A US model, not a US chip, became the controlled item.

China answered in kind on 2026-10-16. Its Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) and Ministry of Science and Technology published Announcement No. 48, a draft export catalogue of 40 technologies, 23 of them AI, with 14 prohibited and 26 restricted across all sectors ([MOFCOM](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2026/art_0a31a3c9ac404f7a9b0e917ce3412997.html)). MOFCOM's pages confirm those counts and the comment period to 2026-11-15. They also confirm an interim pre-export registration rule that binds now, including for exports already under way. They do not confirm how the 23 AI items split between prohibited and restricted. The 2-trillion-parameter threshold is press-only: Bloomberg reported it, and neither the notice nor the spokesperson's Q&A states any threshold ([MOFCOM Q&A](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcjd/art/2026/art_b11d5c1e9374421e93e85dc87eba6e38.html)). The Q&A puts application programming interface (API), app and hosted-software use outside the controls and overseas local deployment inside them. It proposes automatic licensing for open-source releases. [[2026-10-17]] carried the headline. No first-party US reaction was found by the end of the window.

The rule matters for the open-weight story above. Chinese labs supplied 69% of OpenRouter's tokens in its latest reported week, per the same 2026-09-22 newsletter ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/data/newsletter/the-token-state-of-play)).

The chip channel has nearly closed from the Chinese side. Washington licenses Nvidia's H200 for China, but per Bloomberg Tax, sales were under 1% of Nvidia's data center revenue in the quarter to 2026-07-26, and guidance assumes no China data center revenue ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/nvidia-steps-back-into-china-market-with-first-h200-chip-sales)). The 2026-09-25 summit created a "Super Intelligence Dialogue" and an incident channel, and left chips out. Trump said "When you're leading, you don't open it up to each other" ([UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/US/2026/09/26/White-House-Trump-Xi-agree-super-intelligence-dialogue/1951790465468/)). The US had named six Chinese labs in a distillation advisory on 2026-09-08 ([[2026-09-09]]).

The strongest pushback is the text itself. The exemptions are wide, the parameter threshold is unverified, and the draft is open for comment. The FT reported in July that Beijing was weighing limits on weight downloads, and Beijing did not act then ([Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/china-weighs-export-controls-on-ai-models-including-open-weight-llms/)). The two governments still talk, and the next dialogue is due by November.

Read: this edition reads the Chinese draft as leverage held in reserve, not a wall, and the test is whether the final catalogue requires approval for overseas hosting of models like Kimi and Qwen.

## Other threads

**Across five smaller threads, AI costs are moving onto buyers, publishers and insurers, and the national statistics cannot yet show the gain that would pay for them.**

GPU prices. Rental indexes for graphics processing units (GPUs) disagree by about 50%, because they define price differently: public on-demand list prices, neocloud rates and one-year contracts are different products. All of them point up in 2026 after falls in 2025. Uvik's median on-demand H100 list price was \$4.20 an hour on 2026-10-07 ([Uvik](https://uvik.net/blog/gpu-price-index/)). SemiAnalysis put one-year contracts at \$2.35 in March, up from \$1.70 in October 2025 ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-great-gpu-shortage-rental-capacity)). This cuts against the "cheap tokens" half of the thesis: price per task falls, but the compute underneath does not.

Publisher traffic. Chartbeat data in the Reuters Institute's 2026 trends report shows Google organic traffic to over 2,500 news sites fell 33% globally and 38% in the US between November 2024 and November 2025 ([Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-01/Newman%20-%20Trends%20and%20Predictions%202026%20FINAL.pdf)). The report says it does not establish how much AI Overviews caused. Google disputes the story: Liz Reid wrote on 2025-08-06 that total organic click volume is "relatively stable", with no figures ([Google](https://blog.google/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/)).

Copyright as a cost. The \$1.5B Bartz v. Anthropic settlement won final approval on 2026-07-20, at about \$3,000 per work ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/)). That prices pirated books, not lawfully gathered data. The next price is the fair-use question in NYT v. OpenAI, where cross-motions were filed on 2026-09-04 ([[2026-09-18]]).

Productivity measurement. A Federal Reserve Finance and Economics Discussion Series (FEDS) Note of 2026-07-17 finds no large aggregate productivity signal from AI. It says there is "no established consensus" on how to identify AI investment in national accounts ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-ai-buildout-and-the-economy-publicly-available-data-to-assess-ais-impact-20260717.html)).

Insurance exclusions.
