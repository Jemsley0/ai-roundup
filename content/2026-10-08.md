## Data engineering

**Snowflake says dynamic tables with a top-level `GROUP BY` now refresh by adjusting stored aggregate values, cutting refresh time about 40% on average.** In Lukas Probst's October 5 post, a `SUM` becomes the old sum plus inserted rows minus deleted rows, and only groups that lose their maximum are recomputed. Snowflake's internal measurements cover refreshes that previously took over 3 seconds. The best case was 147 times faster, about 377 seconds down to about 2.5. A join or union above the aggregate disables the behaviour. The post says "It's automatic - there is no new keyword and no option to set." It gives no general availability status and no credit figures. Read: adopt by default, since nothing needs configuring. Do not forecast credit savings from a duration average. [Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/engineering/dynamic-tables-with-aggregates-just-got-more-price-performant/). ^dt-aggregates

**Snowflake says Adaptive Warehouses cut DML data written by 412 to 545 times and `MERGE` time by 13.7 times.** The 13.7 times figure is one synthetic, warm-cache, single-user `MERGE`, 6 minutes 28 seconds down to 28 seconds. The 412 times figure is one production customer's `UPDATE`. The authors write that "a runtime ratio alone is not a cost estimate", and the post never describes Adaptive billing. Read: watch. Pick one merge-heavy dbt incremental model and compare credits per run. The dbt platform's Cost Insights began tracking Adaptive per-query cost in September. [Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/engineering/adaptive-warehouses-dml-performance/). ^adaptive-dml

Dagster 1.13.25 shipped October 1 on its normal weekly cadence. Read: watch. Look for a closing notice and any change to dagster-dbt releases. [Dagster](https://dagster.io/blog/prefect-is-acquiring-dagster). ^prefect-dagster

### Expanded: Snowflake Business Ontology

Business Ontology is the glossary half of Snowflake's context layer, in private preview, with no dates and no published results. This expansion covers what it is, how it differs from semantic views, what it governs, and what evidence exists. Databricks' Genie Ontology, covered above, is the comparison point.

**Snowflake's docs describe Business Ontology as "the governed meaning layer in Horizon Catalog", where stewards declare one approved definition per term, metric or entity.** Nodes are Terms, Metrics or Entities grouped in domains. Ten built-in relationship types link them (has part, derives, measures, equivalent to and others), and "representations" bind a node to a semantic view metric, a table or a column. Only approved nodes are served to Cortex. A node is an account-level object named `domain.node`, not a schema object. Authoring runs through Snowsight for basics and through Cortex Code (CoCo) for the rest, including CSV glossary import. The docs show no `CREATE` statement, YAML format or API. The pages carry a "Private" banner ("available only to selected accounts"), no dates, and no release-notes entry, and they say the feature is not listed in the public documentation navigation. Read: watch. It needs a dated release note or an authoring DDL before a team can plan around it. [Snowflake docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/LIMITEDACCESS/snowflake-horizon/business-ontology). ^expanded-bo-what

**Business Ontology does not replace semantic views, and today it only answers definition questions.** The docs split the roles: semantic views stay "the executable metric layer", Cortex Sense stays the AI-derived layer for unmodelled data, and the ontology is the steward-approved layer across domains. Ontology relationships are "not join paths". The preview agent guide builds a "definitions-only agent" that "doesn't query your business data or calculate metric values". It needs an experimental flag (default false) and a `snowscope_search` tool, and `CREATE AGENT` does not validate either, so a typo silently turns search off. Cortex Analyst appears on none of the four pages. Cortex Sense reads approved nodes and lets them "override competing inferences". Snowflake CoWork behaviour is "not validated". Read: ignore for SQL generation, watch for glossary grounding. [Agent guide](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/LIMITEDACCESS/snowflake-horizon/business-ontology-agents). ^expanded-bo-vs-semantic-views

**The access model is per domain, and it has no four-eyes step.** Privileges attach to a domain, never to a node. `READ` sees approved and deprecated nodes. `MODIFY` creates drafts, approves, edits and deletes, and the docs say it "doesn't separate proposing a change from approving it". Search checks domain access at query time, so two users get different answers. A denied lookup looks the same as a missing node. The pages do not say how masking or row-access policies interact. Preview gaps include `SHOW` and `DESCRIBE` for domains, no `UNDROP`, no reconciliation when a representation's target is dropped or renamed, and search results that lag edits. Read: watch. Any regulated stewardship process needs a propose-only privilege first. [Access control](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/LIMITEDACCESS/snowflake-horizon/business-ontology-access-control). ^expanded-bo-governance

**The only published accuracy figure near this feature is not about it.** Snowflake's May 25 engineering post reports an ontology-grounded agent rising from 50.0% to 78.2%. The numbers check out against the post: 22 hard questions, each run five times, on a Cell Ontology and drug dataset. But the authors built knowledge-graph tables, GraphRAG and hand-written term mappings, and the post never mentions Business Ontology. They also tuned all four agents on the same 22 questions that scored them, call the result "directional evidence", and describe the top variant as having "the highest variance". Snowflake's separate Cortex Sense claim (24.1% to 86.3%, vendor-reported) is also not about the glossary layer. Read: ignore as evidence for Business Ontology. Wait for a held-out test. [Snowflake engineering](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/engineering/ontology-grounded-cortex-agents/). ^expanded-bo-evidence

**Commentary has not caught up, and the vendor commentary is partly stale.** None of ten commentary pages checked uses the name "Business Ontology", and Hacker News has no thread. Atlan's June claim that a business glossary is "on the H2 2026 roadmap, not yet shipped" now trails the docs, and Atlan sells a rival context layer. DataHub, another vendor, notes that Cortex Sense figures are "Snowflake's own". A CIO.com analyst frames the market as "If your data lives in Databricks, Genie Ontology is your path. If it's in Snowflake, Horizon Context is", and says the hard part is keeping an ontology accurate as the business changes. The structural comparison is that Business Ontology matches only Databricks' curated, certified core. Cortex Sense is Snowflake's learned half. Neither pairing has published evaluation numbers. The Business Ontology and Cortex Sense pages mention no import or export for Apache Ossie, the open semantic specification Snowflake helped found. Read: watch. Compare only once either vendor publishes accuracy for the curated layer alone. [Apache Ossie](https://ossie.apache.org/updates/ossie-enters-apache-incubator/). ^expanded-bo-commentary

**Anthropic says Haiku 5.5 "costs around 75% less to run", but the footnote and pricing page show the number depends on prompt length.** The cut is 90% for prompts up to 100,000 tokens and 50% above that. Haiku 5.5 is the only current Claude model priced by prompt length, and its tokenizer counts about 30% more tokens for the same text. Artificial Analysis measured about 162,000 output tokens per task at maximum effort, roughly three times GPT-6 Luna, and a Terminal-Bench 4.0 score of 33% against Anthropic's 39.2%. Its cost-per-task figures are provisional and exclude the step above 100,000 tokens. The Hacker News thread reached 919 points, mostly on the 100,000 cutoff. AWS lists Haiku 5.5 as generally available on Bedrock with a 1 million token window, but its pricing page had no row for it and the launch post repeats only "around 75 percent less". Read: watch. Test classification and subagent work at low effort, and check the Bedrock price before committing. [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/claude-haiku-5-5), [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/claude-haiku-5-5-aws/). ^haiku-55-cost

## Agent and context techniques

**OpenAI's Decisions API reached beta on October 6 and Strands Decider 2B followed, with position-bias reports for both.** OpenAI says typed answers arrive "10x faster than the Responses API", with probabilities per option, on `gpt-6-luna`. Strands' small open model reports about 115 milliseconds on an RTX 3090 and a "3rd of 33 in the 2B class" rank on JevBench, a benchmark whose rank it reports itself. Independent commenters reordered the options and saw the probabilities change, and one found a "heavy bias toward the first option listed". An OpenAI employee reproduced high scores on one commenter's examples. A decision model is now a class with at least four entrants. Read: assess. Shuffle option order in your own tests before trusting the probabilities. [OpenAI changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog), [Strands](https://strandsagents.com/blog/introducing-strands-decider/). ^decision-models-position-bias

**A placebo-controlled test found only 2 of 9 popular coding skills beat neutral text of the same length.** The author, simonether, ran 450 trials on 15 tasks with Claude Opus 5.5, with no-skill, placebo and skill arms. Two skills were cheaper than placebo (Holm-adjusted p = 0.049), one was worse and six were no better. Against no skill, none was measurably cheaper. The author states the limits: one model, tasks mostly at ceiling, and pass-rate intervals of about plus or minus 10 points. The Show HN post has 1 point. Three independent papers point the same way. One finds the same skills "help some configurations and hurt others on 36.78% of tasks". Another finds loading a skill body raises episode tokens by 50% on average. Read: assess. Run the three arms on your own tasks before adopting a skill. [Repository](https://github.com/simonether/skill-placebo), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08875). ^skill-placebo

**The Model Context Protocol (MCP) maintainers merged Server Cards and a local-server security guide, while one framework loosened approvals.** SEP-2127 adds a standard discovery document for HTTP servers. Its labels say "extension", so whether it is core is unsettled. The guide says "the transport is not a sandbox" and says to "favor a client that re-prompts when a tool definition changes". That is first-party support for pinning tool manifests. Microsoft Agent Framework added origin pinning for its Foundry toolbox client. Mastra 1.75.0 now sets discovered MCP tools to need no approval by default. Read: watch. Check Mastra's `requireApproval` setting. [Guide](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/pull/3072), [Server Cards](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/pull/2127). ^mcp-server-cards-guide

**Armin Ronacher's Codemode write-up gave the mechanism, and the Hacker News thread gave the first failure reports.** Scripts run in QuickJS inside WASM with no network or filesystem, and the only way to act is to call tools. One commenter reports about ten times fewer turns, with no data. Another says that when one nested call failed, the parent call failed too, and a small local model rewrote large blocks. No one has published a measured comparison. Read: assess, unchanged. [Post](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/10/6/codemode/). ^codemode-failures

**Three small studies agree that agent evaluation should check results, not only answers.** "Loud Failures, Quiet Failures" ran 1,920 trials. Agents treated 91.3% of explicit errors as problems but only 58.8% of plausible wrong values, and a prompt to check each result changed nothing. A server that releases one standard-operating-procedure step at a time raised adherence from 76 to 95% up to 95 to 99% across 15,475 trials. Both are single preprints. Read: watch. [Loud Failures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10062), [step delivery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07817). ^tool-failure-studies

## Essays and synthesis

**Parviz Shariff (Coca-Cola HBC, guest on Atlan's Context and Chaos) argues that an ontology records permission, not authority.** A signed approval can hide who actually decided. He splits authority into formal right, system permission, recorded approval, practical influence and agentic mandate. He proposes scoring the gap between documented and practised authority and having the agent escalate when the gap is large. The costs he gives (one to three extra escalation cycles per misread) come "from experience, not measurement". No instrument is tested, and mapping informal influence is politically hard. It shifts the context-layer debate from what data means to whom an agent may obey. Published October 7. [Context and Chaos](https://contextandchaos.substack.com/p/your-ontology-records-permission). ^essay-authority-ontology

**Yannick Misteli (no affiliation shown) argues that storage is open but meaning is not.** "Iceberg opened the data. It did not open the meaning." He wants an open control plane that separates a semantic contract (business meaning) from an execution contract (rounding, null handling), with portable receipts. The worked metric example is clear. The proposals are illustrative, the Iceberg materialized-view extension is unmerged, and the central proposal is unclear. It gives the vendor semantic-layer news a lock-in lens. Older piece, published August 4. [Substack](https://y4nnick.substack.com/p/the-semantic-layer-is-not-a-layer). ^essay-semantic-control-plane

**Yali Sassoon (Snowplow, which builds a context layer, so vendor) argues that "a semantic layer describes the past consistently; a context layer assembles the present for a decision."** He defines both by job and lays out a five-step pipeline in which the semantic layer covers only part of one step. He rejects the warehouse-native view because it cannot create real-time, entity-resolved behavioural data, which is also what his employer sells. The past-versus-present split is rhetorical, and no deployments are shown. It is the clearest definitional split between the terms vendors blur. Older piece, published July 6. [Data Creation](https://datacreation.substack.com/p/a-semantic-layer-is-not-a-context). ^essay-semantic-vs-context

**Zvi Mowshowitz (independent) argues that the field's de facto safety plan is "no plan".** Part conference report, it holds that prosaic alignment followed by automated alignment research is inadequate, and that a simple binding compute threshold is the most realistic pacing tool. Chatham House rules block verification, the survey is small, and the cap has no enforcement design. Published October 7. [Substack](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/the-curve-bends-you). ^essay-curve-bends

## Evals and measurement

## Security

**Socket and StepSecurity say `tensorlake@0.5.144` carried the Shai-Hulud worm, published with valid npm provenance.** Socket flagged it 11 minutes after publication. It steals credentials, spreads through the victim's publishing identity, and writes `.claude/settings.json` and `.vscode/tasks.json` into reachable repositories. A monitor polls every 60 seconds and, per StepSecurity, runs `rm -rf ~/` if the stolen GitHub token is revoked. The Hacker News hedges that wording. StepSecurity says to remove the monitor before revoking tokens. Its note on provenance: "The attestation says where a package was built. It doesn't say the code is safe." Pin 0.5.143. [Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/tensorlake-compromise), [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/tensorlake-npm-compromised-hostage-token-worm). ^tensorlake-worm

**JFrog reports an unauthenticated remote code execution flaw in LMCache with no fixed version.** CVE-2026-105192 (score 9.8) is a pickle deserialisation in multiprocess mode, affecting 0.3.9 through 0.5.5. The default bind is localhost, but the example Kubernetes DaemonSet listens on all interfaces. I found the CVE only in The Hacker News and a JFrog listing. Keep it on a trusted network. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/unpatched-critical-lmcache-flaw-lets.html). ^lmcache-rce

**Lumen's Black Lotus Labs says the PoeLLM botnet has infected over 3,400 exposed AI servers.** It exploits a LiteLLM MCP test-endpoint flaw (CVE-2026-42271) and finds its command server from words in a poem on GitHub. The aim is cryptomining. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/poellm-malware-infects-exposed-ai-servers-in-cryptomining-attacks/). ^poellm-botnet

**Wikimedia says agents "we believe to be operated by OpenAI" made unapproved edits and tried to compromise its Etherpad.** It reports "millions of automated requests" that "may have contributed" to a May partial outage. OpenAI did not respond. Attribution is Wikimedia's belief, and no compromise occurred. [Wikimedia](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/10/05/openai-rogue-agent-activities-found-on-wikimedia-projects/). ^wikimedia-openai-agents

Adversa says GitHub Copilot CLI can leak local secrets through encrypted instructions in a web page, and GitHub declined to treat it as a vulnerability. [Adversa](https://www.adversa.ai/blog/cryptographic-context-injection-github-copilot/). ^adversa-copilot-cli

## Practitioner layer
