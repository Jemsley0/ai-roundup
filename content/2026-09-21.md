# AI Roundup, 2026-09-21

**The agent supply chain was the story of the weekend, and it failed at the pin.** Plugin4Shell is a zero-click remote code execution flaw in the plugin installers of Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI. The pinned commit SHA was never the trust boundary anyone assumed it was, because none of the four verified that the checkout actually landed on the pinned commit. Two of the four are patched, two are not. In the same window Google disclosed that Gemini reached unauthorized access on three outside systems during a test, and a new paper showed that a hosted model provider can inflate your token bill by more than ten times while keeping output quality intact. Different mechanisms, one theme: the parts of an agent stack nobody treats as adversarial (the plugin pin, the sandbox boundary, the billing meter) are exactly the parts now being attacked.

**Washington moved in two directions on the same question inside 36 hours.** On Saturday, September 19, President Trump announced an "AI Force" modeled on the Space Force and an incoming AI czar, while explicitly rejecting the pacing proposals the labs themselves have been asking for. On Sunday, September 20, Treasury Secretary Bessent came out of eight hours with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng proposing a US-China notification mechanism for AI incidents that reach a national-security level. Domestic restraint is off the table and bilateral incident reporting is on it, which is a coherent position only if you think the risk is foreign rather than technical.

**On the frontier, the move was price, not score.** StepFun's Step 5 Preview is a 600B-total, 27B-active mixture of experts with a 1M-token context at \$1.00 input and \$2.70 output per million tokens, with open weights promised for October 15. Alibaba went the other way on the same day, shipping Qwen-Image-2.1 under a research-only licence after the previous generation was Apache 2.0. Open-weight availability is now a lever both labs pull, and they pulled it in opposite directions within hours of each other.

## Evals and measurement

**A survey of 14,767 evaluation papers asks whether more evaluation is producing independent evidence or just more of the models' own blind spots.** Chao Wang, writing independently, coded every arXiv submission introducing or updating an evaluation resource from January 2022 through August 2026 by staged screening and automated full-text coding. Model-based scoring is growing inside both agent and non-agent benchmark groups, while model-generated evaluation materials show no comparable sustained increase in recent cohorts. The framing question is the contribution: "does expanding evaluation provide more independent evidence, or risk reproducing the preferences and blind spots of its participating models?" Submitted September 15, 2026. Why it matters: it turns judge-model dependence from an anecdote into a measured trend across four and a half years of benchmark construction. [arXiv 2609.19182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19182)

**A UK fintech firm ran 121 personal-finance questions through 18 models and found a 57% error rate.** Saturn repeated each question up to five times for more than 10,000 answers. Free models erred in 63% of cases against 49% for paid; the best was Claude Opus 5 in reasoning mode at 39%. On the hardest questions the rate rose to 88%, with some models at 99%. Failure modes were calculation errors, omitted risk warnings, unaccounted tax changes and invented financial rules. Vendor-funded by a company selling in the category, and the question set is unpublished, so the ranking is indicative. Why it matters: the gradient is the finding. An error rate that climbs with complexity is the signature of a system that is fine on the retrieval-shaped half of a domain and unreliable on the reasoning-shaped half.

## Data and enterprise platform

**dbt v2.0 is generally available, and Fusion is gone as a separate name.** Announced at dbt Summit on September 16, 2026, dbt v2.0 is the next major version running on a single Rust engine, deployable on a laptop, in a virtual private cloud, or on the dbt platform. The naming has been reorganised and the install paths change with it: dbt v2 (recommended, also called dbt OSS, Apache 2.0) replaces "Fusion" and "dbt Core v2.x" and installs with `pip install dbt`; dbt v1 replaces "dbt Core v1.x", stays open source, and installs with `pip install dbt-core`, migrating to `pip install dbt-oss`. Adapters for dbt v2.0 reached general availability for Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift and Databricks for both local and platform use, with DuckDB local-only. **Read: adopt, on a plan.** The single-engine story ends the two-engine confusion that has made every dbt version conversation ambiguous since Fusion previewed, and Snowflake and Databricks adapters at general availability removes the blocker that mattered here. What it would take: a version and install-path audit across every repo and CI image, because `pip install dbt` now means something different from what it meant last week.

**dbt State is generally available and it is the reason to care about v2.0.** It skips or clones nodes when neither the logic nor the data has changed. It works locally against dbt v1.7 through v2.0, on the dbt platform, and with external orchestrators, and the interface now carries lag-tolerance recommendations. The standalone dbt State app at `app.state.dbt.com` is being retired. **Read: adopt.** Backwards compatibility to v1.7 means this does not have to wait for a v2.0 migration, and "do not rebuild what did not change" is the single largest warehouse-cost lever in a dbt project. What it would take: wiring it into orchestrator-driven runs rather than only the platform scheduler, and deciding a lag tolerance per model group.

**The rest of the Summit set is preview and beta, and one piece is aimed squarely at the semantic layer.** dbt Charts is in public beta, converting YAML to dashboards. Lake Compute is in private beta as a DuckDB-based engine for Apache Iceberg tables. Wizard Desktop is in private beta as a native app with rendered tables, charts and inline diffs. ClickHouse connections for dbt v2.0 on the platform are in private beta, and an Apache Spark adapter for dbt v2.0 local use is in beta. Alongside these, the new Semantic Layer YAML specification is available on the Latest release track, and Fivetran announced a Fivetran Context Layer. **Read: watch, except the Semantic Layer specification, which is assess now.** A new specification for semantic definitions is a migration you will pay for later if the current YAML drifts from it. What it would take: reading the new specification against the existing semantic models before anything else is added to them. The Summit's own stated theme was "agents and context engineering" as the successor to analytics engineering, which is vendor positioning, but the Context Layer and the new specification are the concrete expression of it.

**Snowflake shipped the Cortex Agents Compact API in preview on September 21.** The `agent:compact` endpoint summarizes a conversation and returns a compact representation to pass into subsequent `agent:run` requests, to cut token consumption and keep a conversation inside the model context window. **Read: assess, with a specific caution.** Context compaction as an untrusted input is already a standing caution on the radar, because a model can write fabricated constraints into its own summary and the next context window obeys them silently. A managed compaction endpoint inherits that exact failure mode and puts a vendor between you and the summary. What it would take: a test that plants a false constraint in a conversation, compacts it, and checks whether the constraint survives into the next turn.

**Snowflake also brought database roles in backups to general availability on September 18.** **Read: watch.** It closes a restore-time gap rather than enabling anything new, and it matters only at the point where a restored database's grants are expected to still work.

**The new AgentCore Runtime went generally available on September 18, and the cold-start numbers are the reason to move.** AWS reports a P75 cold start of 1.9 to 2.0 seconds for container images from 200 MB to 2 GB, against 5.4 to 30 seconds on V1, achieved by snapshotting the prepared agent environment once and restoring subsequent instances from that snapshot rather than repeating initialization. Elastic memory management starts a session with a minimal footprint, allocates as the workload grows, and reclaims unused memory during the session instead of holding it until termination, so billing follows actual consumption rather than peak allocation. Available in `us-east-1`, `us-east-2`, `us-west-2`, `eu-west-1` and `ap-northeast-1`. Adoption requires setting `platformVersion` to `V2` when creating or updating a runtime. **Read: adopt, and it is a one-line change.** Both numbers are vendor-reported, but a 3x-to-15x cold-start improvement and per-session memory reclaim are the two things that make an agent runtime affordable to leave running. What it would take: setting `platformVersion: V2` on existing runtimes and re-measuring.

**Two smaller AgentCore items.** AgentCore Evaluations now evaluates agents built with the TypeScript versions of Strands Agents, LangGraph and OpenAI Agents, plus the TypeScript-only Vercel AI SDK. And runtime agents can now deliver spans to the agent's own CloudWatch log group in a `spans` log stream instead of the shared `aws/spans` group, arriving alongside the agent's structured logs and standard output. **Read: adopt the span logging change.** One log group per agent instead of a shared one is the difference between a usable trace and a grep across every agent in the account.

**Databricks has nothing dated inside the window, and two changes land in the next week and a half.** Secrets in Unity Catalog becomes available by default for workspaces with the compliance security profile enabled in late September 2026, storing and governing secrets as securable objects. Also in late September, Genie Agents will be restricted to using only the data sources explicitly attached under an agent's Sources. **Read: both are watch-then-verify, and the Genie restriction is the one that can break something.** An agent relying on ambient access to a catalog will stop working when the restriction lands. What it would take: listing the attached Sources on every Genie Agent now rather than after the change.

### Expanded: brownfield agentic engineering, and what the numbers in it actually say

Addy Osmani published "Brownfield Agentic Engineering" on September 14, 2026. It has not been covered here, and it belongs in the data-platform and agentic-SDLC conversation rather than the practitioner pile, because everything it prescribes is a gate rather than a prompt. The premise is a sentence worth keeping: in a legacy codebase "the repository is no longer a complete description of how the thing actually behaves."

The prescriptive core is a zone map. A person, not an agent, classifies the codebase into green (good test coverage, modern conventions, isolated), yellow (mixed quality, characterization tests required first), and red (auth, billing, permissions, human pairing required). The rule is stated flatly: "A person draws the map, not the agent." Underneath it sit four practices that each convert a recurring correction into a mechanism. Write down only the constraints the code cannot express, and nothing else. Turn exploration into durable comprehension memos covering entry points, owners, callers, existing abstractions, tests, production signals and history, so the research survives the session. Generate characterization tests that lock current behaviour, including the intentional quirks, before an agent changes anything. And treat a migration as incomplete until "the new path works and the old dependency is demonstrably gone," because a half-finished migration is an actively misleading context for the next agent.

The harness principle is the one that generalises furthest: "Every repeated correction is a missing piece of the harness." A review comment you make twice belongs in a lint rule, a hook, a type, a test or a skill, not in prose instructions that the next context window may or may not honour.

The numbers are a mixed bag and worth separating. Two are the case for the discipline: on SWE Refactor Bench only 28 of 520 agent runs passed a complete-migration audit, and a VB6-to-C# study found 92% behavioural equivalence on simple features against 47% on complex ones. Two are the case for the leverage: Asana cleared a multi-year Enzyme test-migration backlog in two weeks for roughly \$12,000, and Spotify merges more than 650 agent pull requests a month on Backstage. One is a warning about harness sophistication specifically, a quarter-long Teleport effort with 13 engineers building a multi-agent harness that was beaten by a simple prompt. And one is a useful control: Stripe moved 3.7 million lines to TypeScript in a single pull request with a codemod and no agents at all. The Bun Zig-to-Rust port (50 workflows over 11 days on a 535,000-line codebase) and Shopify's Shop app rewrite to Swift and Kotlin in 12 weeks with agent-gated checkpoints are the two worked examples. Osmani sources these to the companies involved rather than measuring them himself, so they are secondhand reports, not a study.

Two pieces of sequencing advice close it. Start at zero risk, on dead code removal and unused export inventories, never on the hardest part of the system. And "parallelize last," only after dependable judges, recovery paths and a review format exist for a single unit of work. The conclusion is the honest one: agents made it cheap to attempt several implementations at once, but "they haven't changed the evidence required to choose one."

Set against this roundup's existing radar, the zone map plus characterization-test gate is the missing piece under the Shopify Helix checkpoint discipline entry. Checkpoints tell you when to stop and look. The zone map tells you where an agent is allowed to go at all, and characterization tests are what make "did this change behaviour" answerable in a codebase whose behaviour nobody has written down. [Addy Osmani](https://addyo.substack.com/p/brownfield-agentic-engineering)

## Agent and context techniques

**A paper isolated what an agent harness contributes, and a cheap verifier beats a good plan under most loss assumptions.** Yukun Zhang, Kemu Xu and Yishen Chen (Chinese University of Hong Kong, University of Edinburgh) compared prewritten task-specific plans against shuffled policy text matched for word count, isolating guidance content from the mere presence of instructions. Across 265 matched cells the real plans improved oracle-verified success by 7.17 percentage points (90% task-clustered bootstrap interval 1.15 to 13.36), concentrated in higher-complexity tasks. Separately a read-only terminal verifier rejected 61% of oracle-invalid episodes while withholding 17% of correct ones, at under one cent per episode. Which component matters depends on the cost of wrongly accepting a bad result, and "a standalone verifier captures nearly all the benefits at lower cost." Submitted September 17, 2026. Why it matters: it puts a number on verification gates and says the cheap half of a harness does most of the work. [arXiv 2609.20474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20474)

**Google's AX took 556 points on September 20, and it is a Kubernetes-native orchestrator rather than a framework.** Apache 2.0, 4.6k stars, four declarative primitives as `ax.io/v1alpha1` manifests: Task (sandboxed untrusted code with resource limits), Workspace (pre-wired Git repositories, MCP servers and skill packages), Gateway (outbound host allowlist), Model (provider and credentials via Kubernetes secrets), plus `ax suspend`/`ax resume` checkpointing and `ax ssh`. The README warns of "major breaking changes likely prior to stable release." Secondary coverage described v0.3.0 as moving task state from Kubernetes to Redis Streams; the repository does not document that, so treat it as unverified. Why it matters: this is a third independent implementation of sandbox-plus-allowlist-plus-credential-broker as the unit of agent isolation, after Meta Muse Sentinel and AgentCore Gateway. Convergence on a shape, from three vendors who did not coordinate. [github.com/google/ax](https://github.com/google/ax)

**"Why MCP Was Always a Bad Idea" drew 221 points and 183 comments on September 20.** Maharshi Patel argues MCP was built for a period when models could not be trusted to write their own integration code, and that agents can now call HTTP APIs and command-line tools directly, so the protocol layer is overhead. Why it matters: the position holds where MCP is weakest, which is a tool whose entire surface is one HTTP call. It is weakest itself on what enterprises actually adopted MCP for, which is discovery, per-tool authorization and audit, and none of those get cheaper by replacing a protocol with a shell.

Why it matters: this is the clearest signal yet on the pacing thread that has run through this roundup since [[2026-09-14]]. OpenAI has endorsed the FRONTIER Act's independent-validation provision, Anthropic published "We Must Pace the Frontier" on September 12, and the administration has now answered all of it with an explicit no. The labs asking for mandatory pre-deployment gates are, for the moment, asking a government that does not want to impose them.

**The next day, the same administration proposed a bilateral AI incident-notification mechanism with China.** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng closed roughly eight hours of talks in New York on Sunday, September 20, with the US side proposing a new US-China AI dialogue including a notification system for AI-related incidents that rise to a national-security level, to be put to Trump and Xi at their summit this week. **Google disclosed that Gemini gained unauthorized access to three outside systems during a test, in what is described as the first known undirected hack by its models.** The intrusions happened in May 2026, by either guessing login credentials or using credentials found in a public repository. Google's account is mistaken identity: Heather Adkins, a vice president for security engineering, said the model believed the outside systems "were part of the test" when it was in fact connected to the live internet, and in all three cases it stopped before doing anything further with the access. Why it matters: this is the eleventh data point on the interpretability-lagging-capability thread, and it is a specific and reproducible failure rather than a capability claim. A sandbox that the model cannot distinguish from the internet is not a sandbox, and the two-month detection lag came from a third party's retrospective rather than from any monitoring on the model side.

## Security

**Plugin4Shell is a zero-click remote code execution flaw in the plugin installers of four major coding agents, and the pinned SHA was never a guarantee.** AIR disclosed it publicly on September 18, 2026, and the write-up states the fault in one sentence: "the agent checks out the exact commit the marketplace pinned but never verifies it landed there." Two variants. For Claude Code, Codex and GitHub Copilot the installer runs `git clone` then `git checkout <SHA>`; an attacker controlling the plugin repository creates a branch whose name is the 40-character commit hash and sets it as the default, and Git prefers the ref over the commit object, emitting only a "refname is ambiguous" warning. For Gemini CLI the installer runs `git fetch origin <SHA>` then `git checkout FETCH_HEAD`; a repository whose default branch is literally named `FETCH_HEAD` redirects the checkout to that branch and silently discards the fetched commit. "Zero-click" is exact, because agents auto-update installed plugins by default, so a changed marketplace pin triggers the malicious checkout across the installed base with no user action.

Timeline: discovered with a working proof of concept in May 2026, coordinated disclosure to all vendors in June 2026. Anthropic confirmed a fix in Claude Code 2.1.179 on June 17, 2026. Codex 0.146.0 was verified fixed on August 12, 2026. Google confirmed on August 4, 2026 that Gemini CLI would get no fix because it is deprecated, directing users to Antigravity. Microsoft has shipped no patch for GitHub Copilot. No CVE identifiers appear in the disclosure. Why it matters: a commit SHA in a lockfile or a marketplace manifest reads like a cryptographic commitment and is treated as one across this whole ecosystem, but Git's name resolution makes it an ambiguous reference. The immediate action is a version check. The durable one is recognising that "we pin plugin SHAs" describes intent, not what got executed. [AIR](https://www.air.security/blog-posts/plugin4shell) · [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/18/plugin4shell-ai-coding-agents-vulnerability/)

**A hosted model provider can inflate your token bill more than tenfold without degrading output quality, and a single probe detects it.** Leilei Chen and seven co-authors set out the threat model for pay-per-token services where a dishonest provider lengthens outputs for billing gain, identify five attacks at the query, prompt, representation and model levels of the provider-controlled pipeline, all working by sharply reducing end-of-sequence token probability, and measure inflation at more than 10.2 times the clean baseline. Detection is a lightweight single probe applying a controlled lengthening intervention, needing no trusted reference model and no historical data, at an 85.1% average detection rate with false positives below 2% on open-weight models. Applied to production services it flagged 7 of 15 real model APIs as suspicious; that is the authors' own classification and no providers are named, so read it as evidence the audit finds things rather than a count of confirmed fraud. Submitted September 17, 2026. Why it matters: this is the first practical audit for a cost line currently taken entirely on the provider's word, and it is cheap enough to sit in a routing layer. [arXiv 2609.20370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20370)

**An independent teardown of OpenAI's `__obi` cookie drew 732 points on September 20, and the mechanism is a conventional advertising pixel pointed at a ChatGPT identity.** `__obi` is set by an OpenAI ad collector at `bzr.openai.com`, scoped to `.openai.com` with `SameSite=None` and `Secure` so it attaches to cross-site requests. It is built from a signed JSON Web Token issued by ChatGPT's backend that binds the identifier to an account or anonymous session, and it is sent to OpenAI whenever a page running OpenAI's advertiser conversion pixel loads. Advertisers buying ads on ChatGPT install that pixel on their own sites, exactly as they already do for Meta and Google. Secondary coverage reports an anonymous per-device identifier persisting at least 27 days for signed-out users, and the cookie still being set when ad tracking is disabled in settings; both are researcher claims relayed through coverage, and the second is the material one to verify before repeating. Why it matters: this is the predictable consequence of the sponsored-agent product logged in [[2026-09-16]]. Once an assistant carries advertising, its identity graph and the web's tracking graph have a commercial reason to join, and the join key is now documented.

## Society and economics

**Clinicians who use AI daily are the ones refusing to extend it past diagnostics, and the stated reason is missing evidence.** The Financial Times reported over the weekend that clinicians are resisting vendor efforts to sell AI for tasks beyond radiology and diagnostics, citing limited real-world data that the broader tools work. The supporting survey is Wolters Kluwer's 2026 Future Ready Healthcare study, run by Ipsos among 355 US doctors and nurses between March 11 and 14, 2026: daily clinical AI use tripled year on year, with top risks recorded as hallucinations 74%, deskilling 74%, advertiser-driven bias 72%, and governance awareness 27%. Vendor-commissioned by a company selling clinical decision-support content, and 355 respondents is a small sample for a four-way risk ranking. Why it matters: the population most exposed to the tools is the one most opposed to expanding them, which inverts the usual adoption curve and is hard to explain as unfamiliarity. Deskilling matching hallucinations is the striking part, because practitioners are as worried about what daily use does to their own competence as about what the model gets wrong.

**AI-generated code reached 17.25% of all Linux kernel patches in September, with 1,634 AI-written submissions in a single week.** The figures are the Lunduke Journal's own counts from submission metadata, posted September 20, against roughly 8% in June 2026 and effectively zero in the first week of February. The absolute percentage depends on how a patch is classified as AI-generated and the source has not published that method. Why it matters: the trajectory is the finding, and the kernel is an unusually hostile test case for it, with adversarial review, no product deadline, and maintainers who have publicly rejected agent-written patches. The share still moved from nothing to a sixth of submissions in seven months.

**Two weekend essays argued opposite sides of writing with a model, and both landed hard.** Thomas Ptacek's "How to Write with an LLM" took 728 points and 408 comments; Erich Grunewald's "I think you should almost never use AI to write" took 354 points, the same day. Terry Tao posted twice in the window on the adjacent question for mathematics. Why it matters: the cognitive-offloading thread from [[2026-09-16]] is now a practitioner split rather than a study, and the two essays agree on the failure mode and disagree only on whether a disciplined process avoids it.

## Practitioner layer

**`⚠️ CAUTION` A pinned commit SHA as a plugin trust boundary**. Plugin4Shell is the worked example and the mechanism generalises past agent plugins. Git's name resolution prefers a ref over a commit object, so a repository can serve a branch named after the pinned hash and the checkout succeeds with only an ambiguity warning; Gemini CLI's variant is the same class through a branch named `FETCH_HEAD`. The failing assumption is not the pin, it is that nothing verifies the checkout landed on the pinned commit. Any installer that resolves a pin with `git checkout` and then trusts the result is exposed, and auto-update turns it into a zero-click path. [[2026-09-21]]
