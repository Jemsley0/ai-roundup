## Essays and synthesis

**Tim O'Reilly (founder of O'Reilly Media, a publisher that sells AI and open-source content) argues that Silicon Valley moved from creating value to extracting it, and that AI skipped the value-creating phase.** He blames shareholder-value incentives, not villains, and splits rents into "rising tide" and "robber baron" kinds, set against an "operating economy" and a "betting economy" driven by valuations. The framework ties the AI-bubble, training-data and lost-traffic stories into one incentive account and offers open protocols as the alternative design. The evidence is anecdotal or old, such as a 2021 valuation snapshot, and the alternative has few tested mechanisms. Published November 20, adapted from an October 16 lecture; 793 Hacker News points. [O'Reilly Radar](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/what-happened-to-silicon-valley/). ^essay-oreilly-silicon-valley

**Mani Doraisamy (independent developer) argues that AI writes the code, so the human job is now architecture: pushing back on requirements, designing the data model and constraining the AI to it.** His case is one anecdote: a colleague's vibe-coded install logged through chained calls to fetch a user's email and ID, and the welcome screen took 15 seconds to load. It gives a concrete failure mode for the claim that engineers become architects, which is that AI follows requirements without pushing back. It is one incident with no error-rate data, and the root cause is partly process, since nobody profiled before shipping. He concedes the post is "requirements heavy and light on architecture/design". Published November 20; about 620 Hacker News points. [Doraisamy](https://manidoraisamy.com/developer-to-architect.html). ^essay-doraisamy-architects

**Ben Hudson (an Atlan product leader, so a vendor, writing a guest post that promotes Atlan's ai-registry project without disclosing it) argues that AI skills and agent definitions need a registry combining npm's distribution with Apache Iceberg's open specification.** Copies of one skill drift across Claude Code, Codex and Cursor because sharing runs through Slack, and he names three lifecycle questions: consistency, upgrades and deprecation. It extends the registry case Prukalpa Sankar made on November 5 ([[2026-11-06]]). There is no data, no adoption figure and no evidence any harness will read a shared specification, and he concedes one skill behaves differently across models. Published November 19. [Context and Chaos](https://contextandchaos.substack.com/p/managing-ai-artifacts-across-a-company). ^essay-hudson-ai-registry

## Agent and context techniques

**The Model Context Protocol (MCP) maintainers merged the Events extension (specification proposal SEP-3415) on November 19, letting a client subscribe to upstream events so an agent can react "without the user being present".** It is listed as Beta, with poll, push and signed-webhook modes, none mandatory. ChatGPT supports webhook delivery only, and WorkOS (an authentication vendor) warns that "an event subscription is a credential" whose time to live is your revocation window. Anthropic's always-on agents guide (November 13) points the same way, with an event inbox as "the source of truth" and one active worker. Release notes from OpenAI Agents SDK, Mastra and Pydantic AI add resumable runs. The sources agree on direction and all are vendors, and no one reports running Events in production. [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/seps/3415-events-extension), [WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/mcp-events-chatgpt-subscription-revocation), [Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/building-always-on-agents). ^ac-mcp-events

**Three preprints from November 16 to 18 argue that plain-text or grep-style retrieval is a fairer baseline than dense or lossy memory.** In Embeddings vs. Grep (Xue and co-authors, 13 Hugging Face upvotes), indexing whole files for BM25 shrinks dense retrieval's lead on curated queries from 0.121 to 0.034, and generate-then-grep leads dense retrieval by 0.081. A per-query router missed its own preregistered criterion. GrepMem, an append-only log read by regex, reports 95 to 97% against at most 21% for lossy baselines, on the authors' numbers. All are unreplicated, and none shows a memory product losing. [Embeddings vs. Grep](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20455), [GrepMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25123). ^ac-grep-baseline

**MCP issue #3394, where a lost response on a mutating tool call re-runs the effect, is still open with no visible maintainer reply on November 23.** Participants now favor non-normative guidance first and a separate retry-identity proposal later, and an earlier normative `idempotencyKey` pull request closed on August 23 "on policy grounds". A community test contract merged a pull request on November 22. The Events extension does not close this gap. [GitHub](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/issues/3394). ^ac-mcp-3394

**Neo4j says Relate, an open-source embedded graph database for agents published November 11, installs with one command and answers in "submillisecond" time, with every number its own and no hardware or dataset stated.** Graphiti 0.32.0 (November 23) removed the Kuzu driver and added a Relate driver, plus group isolation: UUID operations now check the group, the one fact a multi-tenant Graphiti user should read. No practitioner report exists. [Neo4j](https://neo4j.com/blog/agentic-ai/meet-relate-by-neo4j-an-embedded-database-for-ai-agents/), [Graphiti](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti/releases/tag/v0.32.0). ^ac-relate-graphiti

**Greptile (a vendor that sells the product it tested) says screenshots plus an accessibility tree help weak models a lot and barely move the strongest.** On 172 OSWorld tasks, one attempt each, GPT-5.2 rose from 15.8% to 52.8% with the tree and element-ID clicks, while GPT-6.1 Sol stayed between 82% and 84%. Scaffolding that helps weak models may add nothing for strong ones. [Greptile](https://www.greptile.com/blog/trex-computer-use). ^ac-greptile-computer-use

## Data engineering

**Databricks says agents plus a governed metric layer lift Genie One accuracy from 64% on tables alone to 95%, on a fictional insurer and 125 questions (vendor-run, no replication).** Agents alone and the governed layer alone each reach 83%, and definition questions go from 69% to 100% with the layer. The useful part is the failure analysis: after a metric changed, agents kept the old formula in 92% of answers outside Genie One, while one change to a certified metric view moved all 12 related answers. That argues for defining measures once in a semantic layer, and it applies to Snowflake semantic views and dbt metrics too. [Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/genie-one-accuracy-why-curated-agents-need-governed-context). ^de-genie-one-accuracy

**Databricks says Genie ZeroOps is in public beta after seeing more than 80,000 customer pipeline issues, and "over half" needed company-specific knowledge to fix.** The post gives no resolution rate, time saved or price. Databricks and Snowflake now describe the same loop, an agent that reads lineage and logs and opens a pull request for a person to review, and Snowflake's under-16-minutes claim is self-reported. [Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/genie-zeroops-now-public-beta-autonomous-agent-fixes-data-pipelines-lessons-80000-customer). ^de-zeroops-public-beta

**Onehouse says Lakegres serves vector search from an index stored inside existing Hudi and Iceberg tables, at 79 milliseconds warm median against 582 for Databricks AI Search on 1 billion vectors.** It is one dataset, vendor-run, with client-side and server-side timing compared, and the author conceded on Hacker News (41 points) that continuous index maintenance was not measured. It is a bid to drop the separate vector store, and the cost case is unproven. [Onehouse](https://www.onehouse.ai/blog/lakegres-vector-search-on-open-table-formats). ^de-lakegres

**Collate says Ontology Studio, a Web Ontology Language (OWL) and Simple Knowledge Organization System (SKOS) semantic layer with a read-only MCP server, launched in Collate 2.1 on November 17, with general availability due later in November.** The knowledge-graph query path stays beta and off by default. The only write-up found is a Futurum page bylined to an AI that the page says no editor reviewed. It takes the W3C standards route, not Apache Ossie. [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/?p=98192). ^de-collate-ontology-studio

## Enterprise platform

**Databricks says traffic from more than 10,000 companies on its Unity Gateway shows single-provider use falling from about 64% in July to 28% by November 2, and open-weight token share rising from 7.3% to 30.9% (vendor-reported).** The sample is firms that already chose a gateway, so some of the fall is selection, which is my inference. The firmer result is the cost spread: median user spend of \$2.49 against \$699.74 at the 99th percentile, which argues for per-user quotas over one shared cap. [Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/introducing-databricks-ai-index-state-enterprise-ai-tokenomics). ^ep-databricks-ai-index

**Databricks (Unity Gateway Insights, November 18) and LiteLLM (Lens Profiles, November 19) both turn agent traces plus gateway spend into task-level cost reports, and both publish only self-estimated or illustrative numbers.** Databricks estimates seven small MCP server bugs wasted \$499K in tokens, and LiteLLM labels its figures "Illustrative demo data". The gateway is becoming where model choice is argued with traffic data, but no one has shown that discovered task categories stay stable enough to compare models. [Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/introducing-unity-gateway-insights), [LiteLLM](https://docs.litellm.ai/blog/lens-profiles). ^ep-gateway-trace-analytics

**Dragos says a privacy flag may govern only the first hop: about two million canary prompts over five months found three hosting providers breached and one Chinese lab fetching secret URLs from more than half the prompts sent to it, including requests flagged no-collection or Zero Data Retention (ZDR).** OpenRouter responded on November 22 by off-boarding three providers and removing 125 endpoints from ZDR eligibility, and says it found no breach of its own systems. Hacker News commenters (294 points) split on whether ZDR is "a marketing ploy" or a routing-layer failure. A gateway policy of "ZDR only" is only as good as the provider list behind it. [Dragos](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens), [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/). ^ep-dragos-canaries

## Security

**StepSecurity, Socket and Wiz say `agent-framework` 1.26.1 (20:35 UTC, 3:35 PM ET) and 1.26.2 (21:08 UTC, 4:08 PM ET) on PyPI installed a startup hook that harvests credentials, on November 17.** It reads environment variables, cloud and Git credential files, then posts them to a `webhook.site` address, and it runs without being imported. Both wheels had no provenance attestation. The root cause is disputed: The Register says an administrator account, while Wiz (updated November 18) says a compromised client certificate and key for Microsoft's release pipeline. Microsoft has confirmed neither, and Wiz saw only three confirmed exfiltrations. [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/malicious-version-of-microsofts-agent-framework-published-to-pypi). ^sec-agent-framework

**StepSecurity says WPPConnect's own trusted-publishing workflow shipped a secret stealer after unsigned commits landed on its default branches (November 15 to 17), with valid provenance.** It runs on import, so `--ignore-scripts` does not stop it, and the release tag was rewritten after researchers opened issues. Single source; at least 155 downloads. It is the third provenance-is-not-safety case in two editions. [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/wppconnect-compromised-malicious-releases-steal-secrets-and-whatsapp-sessions). ^sec-wppconnect

**Wiz says agentic offensive frameworks such as ARTEX are driving a surge in cloud intrusions, with about 20 more related incidents since "Black October" and more than 1,400 exposed ARTEX consoles counted by Censys in early November (vendor-reported).** Wiz separates what its telemetry observed from what it only read in leaked attacker material, and says the activity is not one campaign. [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/agentic-attacks-artex-offensive-ai-frameworks). ^sec-wiz-artex

**StepSecurity, Socket and Wiz say attackers injected a crypto-address swapper and card skimmer into Optimizely-hosted customer scripts, reaching 178 organizations on November 16 and 17.** Optimizely says an attacker exploited a flaw to impersonate users and has published no root cause. [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer). ^sec-optimjack

**Wiz reported three critical vulnerability sets, an OpenJDK "Java4Shell" flaw, two nginx HTTP/2 flaws and 17 PHP flaws, with no exploitation in the wild reported.** Wiz says its own AI harness helped find the nginx flaws (vendor-reported). [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/11/17/critical-java-vulnerability-requires-immediate-patching). ^sec-critical-vulns

## Society and economics

**Anthropic's economics team says 58 customers report hiring cuts more than layoffs: about two-thirds cut hiring because of AI, five pushed out staff who would not adopt it, and two eliminated a role.** One interviewee: "We may not be firing, but we're not hiring."
