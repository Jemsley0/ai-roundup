# Frontier Lab Economics

How frontier labs make and lose money: revenue, losses, pricing power, initial public offering (IPO) and governance structures, and the capital spending and debt behind them.

## Where this stands

Revenue at the top labs is large enough to reshape strategy, and the spending behind it makes any single profit line hard to read. Anthropic told investors it will post a second straight quarter of adjusted operating profit, about \$559M on \$11.5B of Q2 revenue. The Wall Street Journal reported leaked draft S-1 figures that point the other way: \$16.3B of revenue, an \$80.7B net loss and \$9B of compute spend in the first half of 2026, \$450B of mostly noncancellable cloud commitments and a \$2T valuation target. Anthropic then filed its public Form S-1 for a Nasdaq listing under "ANT" and launched its roadshow on November 10 at \$70 to \$78 a share for 825 million Class A shares. TechCrunch reports it priced the offering at \$83 a share on November 18, above the projected range, with 980 million shares and about \$81.3 billion raised, including a \$26 billion non-voting placement from suppliers. Sources differ on the share count and the placement, and this page has not yet checked the filed figures against the leaked ones. Adjusted profit and net loss measure different things. DeepSeek's annualized run rate doubled to roughly \$1B after an August price increase of 2.3x to 4.5x, and ByteDance's first-half revenue rose 30% to \$120B while profit fell under infrastructure spending. All of these figures are company-disclosed, leaked or reported second-hand, and none is audited. The Financial Times said OpenAI told investors its annualised revenue was "approaching \$50 billion", not the roughly \$70 billion in circulation.

Spending and debt set the scale, and the bubble argument has moved to credit. Morgan Stanley says four hyperscalers issued about \$67 billion of bonds since September 30, and PIMCO says AI borrowers are almost half of long-dated investment-grade issuance, though both sell debt products. Goldman Sachs estimates a \$230B annual gap between 2026 hyperscaler AI capital spending and the revenue needed to break even, with debt covering about a third. The International Monetary Fund's October report names a correction in AI-linked assets as a salient risk and counts 14 circular-financing deals since 2023, while saying banks stay resilient under a severe stress test. Anthropic's compute commitments are reported near \$517B through 2029, and the Financial Times reports, headline only via Techmeme, a \$60B Anthropic chip-debt package with Blackstone and a Meta plan to raise \$95B. ByteDance signed a \$29.6B syndicated loan and Z.AI raised about \$5B. Character.AI's Chapter 11 filing shows the small end. A counter-view came from Matteo Raso, an independent blogger, who argues the bubble probably does not exist because the spend is mostly one-time construction, running costs are modest and Alphabet and Meta trade at price-to-earnings ratios in the 20s. His figures are rough and partly uncited, and the thread drew 974 comments, the most disagreement of the window. Alex Tabarrok adds that real tech investment rose about 46% from mid-2023 to mid-2026 against about 3% for other real investment.

Spending keeps rising, and the demand signal is mixed. Microsoft reported \$39.4B of property and equipment additions in fiscal 2027 Q1, up from \$19.4B a year earlier, with Azure up 48%. NVIDIA and Oracle plan to expand Oracle's cloud to "multi-gigawatt scale" with no dollar figure. Ramp says company AI token spend grew 8.9% from September 23 to October 23 while token volume rose 37.1% and Anthropic's share of spend reached 60.5%, with no sample or method.

Listing structure is where the labs diverge, and the public-market window is mixed. Anthropic asked shareholders to approve a share class giving Dario Amodei and six co-founders 50.1% collective voting control. OpenAI pulled its 2026 listing plans, DeepSeek is heading to Shanghai, and Nvidia-backed Firmus scrapped a roughly \$5 billion Australian IPO on October 9, which is one data point on weak demand, not a trend, and Anthropic's above-range pricing is a data point the other way. Labs are also competing harder for customers and facing legal cost. The Information, via The Decoder, reports OpenAI is offering discounts of up to 90 percent to win Anthropic's biggest customers, an unverified figure. OpenAI will sell its models through Google Cloud, Anthropic opened a marketplace of more than 2,000 connectors and plugins, and the New York Times reports, as a headline only, that Elvis Presley's rightsholders sued Anthropic over 274 songs allegedly used in training.

## Open questions

- Do the filed S-1 figures match the WSJ's leaked figures, and how does an \$80.7B first-half net loss sit beside adjusted operating profit of about \$559M in Q2?
- Which share count and which structure for the \$26 billion non-voting supplier placement does a primary filing confirm, given that sources differ?
- What valuation does the \$83 pricing imply, and does it clear the \$1.5T that investors were said to resist against the \$2T draft target?
- Does the supervoting share class survive scrutiny from investors and index providers?
- Does the credit-market pace reported by Morgan Stanley and PIMCO continue, and does anyone outside the debt-product sellers publish the issuance figures?
- Does OpenAI confirm or deny the discounts of up to 90 percent, and does Anthropic's customer base move?
- Does Meta close the \$95B raise on the reported terms, and does a second source confirm the \$35B of public equity?
- Does the Presley rightsholders' suit over 274 songs name a damages figure or a licensing demand?
- Is Anthropic's adjusted operating profit durable against \$450B to \$517B of compute commitments?
- Does a Shanghai listing bring DeepSeek's revenue and price-increase figures under independent audit?
- Does the IMF's circular-financing count of 14 deals change how banks price the next AI data-center loan, and does the Blackstone package get confirmed beyond a headline?
- Does OpenAI's Google Cloud channel shift revenue away from Microsoft and Amazon Web Services, and does OpenAI confirm an annualised revenue figure?
- Does the Firmus withdrawal stay specific to a smaller infrastructure operator, now that Anthropic priced above range?
- Does Ramp publish its sample and method, and does the stall in token spend show up in lab revenue figures?
- Does Microsoft's quarterly capital spend track revenue growth, and does NVIDIA and Oracle's multi-gigawatt plan get a dollar figure?

## 2026-09-25

**Anthropic is asking shareholders to approve a Palantir-style founder-supervoting share class ahead of its initial public offering (IPO).** The proposal would give chief executive Dario Amodei and six co-founders collective 50.1% voting control on roughly 2% equity each, reported by The Information. The structure would lock in founder control regardless of how the IPO prices, and it lands in the same month Anthropic's IPO slipped to November. ([Investing.com, reporting The Information](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-seeks-palantirstyle-voting-control-for-seven-cofounders-ahead-of-ipo-the-information-reports-4916355))

**DeepSeek's annualized revenue run rate doubled to roughly \$1B, self-disclosed by chief executive Liang Wenfeng, driven by API price hikes of 2.3x to 4.5x in August with no measurable drop in demand.** The disclosure came ahead of a \$7.5B funding round and a planned Shanghai Stock Exchange listing. It is real evidence of pricing power in an ostensibly commodity, price-competitive open-weight-adjacent business, though the figures are self-reported at an investor meeting rather than audited. ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/info-1jq5etc-deepseeks-annualized-revenue-hits-1-billion-as-startup-finalizes-7-5-bil/), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/deepseek-doubles-annual-revenue-run-rate-to-1-billion-ahead-of-ipo/))

Source note: [[2026-09-25]]

## 2026-09-21

**Anthropic's initial public offering slipped from a late-September-to-October window to November.** Reported valuation targets run from the \$965B post-money mark set by its \$65B Series H (completed May 2026) up to around \$2T, with the raise itself reported at up to \$100B. Annualized revenue is reported, via the New York Times, to be on track to top \$100B in 2026, against \$9B at the end of 2025 and \$65B in July 2026, a roughly elevenfold increase in nine months with no public-market comparable to argue the price from.

## 2026-09-16

**ByteDance signed a \$29.6B three-year syndicated loan, extendable to five, across more than 28 banks, including ICBC, Bank of China, China Construction Bank, and HSBC.** Coming one day after ByteDance disclosed that first-half profit fell on AI infrastructure capex, the loan is debt raised at a scale that removes any ambiguity about whether the capex cycle continues. ([Crescendo](https://www.crescendo.ai/news/latest-vc-investment-deals-in-ai-startups), [buildfastwithai roundup](https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/ai-news-today-september-16-2026))

## 2026-09-15

**ByteDance's first-half revenue rose 30% to \$120B while profit fell, compressed by AI infrastructure capex.** It is the clearest single data point yet that the capex cycle is now visible in earnings at the largest private operators, not just in projections. ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/bytedances-first-half-profit-drops-20-billion-weighed-ai-spending))

## 2026-09-14

**Anthropic is profitable on an adjusted basis for a second straight quarter, and the revenue curve is steep, though the figures are vendor-disclosed to investors rather than audited.** It projected roughly \$559M in Q2 operating profit on \$11.5B revenue, up from \$4.73B in Q1. Set against that, separate reporting puts its compute commitments at \$517B through 2029, nearly triple previous estimates, which makes the operating-profit line look less like a milestone and more like a rounding detail. ([AI Weekly digest](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today))

**OpenAI pulled its 2026 initial public offering (IPO).** It filed confidentially, but chief executive Sam Altman said "right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public." ([Fortune, via AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today))

**Z.AI raised roughly \$5B, its second major raise in two months after a roughly \$4B July placement, and its shares fell more than 10% on the news.** The structure was a roughly \$2B share placement plus roughly \$3B in zero-coupon convertible bonds due 2027, initially convertible at a 12.5% premium. Stated use of funds: next-generation GLM foundation models, a fully self-training system, and domestic-chip adaptation, a bet on decoupling from both human data curation and Nvidia. ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/09/14/z-ai-completes-around-us5-billion-financing-for-next-generation-glm-models/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/zai-shares-tumble-second-major-raise-two-months-.html), [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-09-14/zai-raises-5-billion-in-share-placement-convertible-bond-sale-102484813.html))
