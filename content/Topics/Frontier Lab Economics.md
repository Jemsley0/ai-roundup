# Frontier Lab Economics

Lab revenue, burn, compute commitments, and capital structure, as disclosed in filings and credible reporting.

## Where this stands

The clearest new fact is Anthropic's own draft initial public offering (IPO) prospectus, reported by Reuters on September 29. It shows 2025 revenue of nearly \$4.6B, up twelve-fold, against a \$42B net loss, of which roughly \$34B is non-cash charges on financing instruments. Forward cloud and compute commitments total \$518B, a figure that lines up with the \$517B compute-commitment number reported two weeks earlier, on September 14, when Anthropic was also telling investors it would post a second straight quarter of positive adjusted operating income (roughly \$559M in Q2 operating profit on \$11.5B revenue, up from \$4.73B in Q1). Those two claims, an adjusted-basis profit and a \$42B GAAP-style net loss, are not necessarily contradictory: the loss is dominated by non-cash charges on financing instruments rather than operating cash burn. But nobody has published a reconciliation between "profitable on an adjusted basis" and "\$42B net loss," and the prospectus is itself a draft reported by Reuters, not a filed S-1.

The capital-structure story around Anthropic's IPO has moved on three separate axes in three weeks. On September 21, the IPO itself slipped from a late-September-to-October window to November, with valuation targets reported from the \$965B post-money mark set by May's \$65B Series H up to around \$2T, and annualized revenue reported by the New York Times as set to top \$100B in 2026, against \$9B at the end of 2025. On September 25, Anthropic asked shareholders to approve a Palantir-style founder supervoting share class, giving Dario Amodei and six co-founders collective 50.1% voting control on roughly 2% equity each, timed directly ahead of the listing. Meanwhile OpenAI pulled its own 2026 IPO plan on September 14, filing confidentially instead, with Altman calling the timing "ill-advised." That leaves Anthropic as the only frontier lab with disclosure-grade financial detail in public view, and OpenAI's comparable burn and compute-commitment numbers still undisclosed.

Outside the two largest US labs, DeepSeek is the sharpest economics data point: on September 25, CEO Liang Wenfeng disclosed that its annualized revenue run rate roughly doubled to \$1B, from roughly \$500M, attributed to an August 2026 price increase that raised API fees 2.3x to 4.5x with no measurable demand drop, ahead of a \$7.5B funding round and a planned Shanghai Stock Exchange listing. That is evidence of real pricing power in an ostensibly commodity, price-competitive market, a different economic story from the compute-commitment scale that dominates the Anthropic and OpenAI coverage, but it shares the same caveat: the figures are self-disclosed by an executive at an investor meeting, not audited.

## Open questions

- Nobody has reconciled Anthropic's mid-September claim of adjusted-basis operating profit with the \$42B net loss shown in the September 29 prospectus reporting. The gap is attributed to non-cash financing charges, but no source has broken that down line by line.
- The Reuters-reported prospectus is a draft. Whether the eventual public filing, once the November IPO proceeds, confirms the \$4.6B revenue, \$42B loss, and \$518B commitment figures is still open.
- OpenAI has no equivalent disclosure. It pulled its IPO and gave no filing-grade revenue, burn, or compute-commitment numbers, so its economics remain comparatively opaque next to Anthropic's.
- DeepSeek's \$1B run rate and 2.3x-4.5x price hike are self-disclosed, not audited. Whether the Shanghai listing process brings standard disclosure and audit requirements that test these numbers is unresolved.
- Whether Anthropic's founder supervoting proposal survives shareholder approval, and what it signals about governance terms other labs seek ahead of their own listings, is undecided.
